# NBA Collective Bargaining Agreement — Plain-Language Summary

> Original summary written for this project, based on widely reported terms of the
> 2023 NBA CBA. Paraphrased for demo/testing use, not an official document.

## Salary cap

The salary cap is a soft cap: teams can exceed it using exceptions (Bird rights,
mid-level exception, bi-annual exception, minimum contracts). The cap is set each
year as a share of Basketball Related Income (BRI), roughly 44.74% of projected
BRI divided by the number of teams, minus benefits.

## Luxury tax

Teams with payroll above the tax line pay a tax on the overage, at rates that
escalate the further over they are and increase again for repeat payers (teams
taxed in at least three of the four prior seasons).

## First and second aprons

The 2023 CBA created two spending thresholds above the tax line. A team over the
first apron loses access to the bi-annual exception and the full mid-level, and
cannot take back more salary than it sends out in a trade. A team over the second
apron cannot aggregate salaries in trades, cannot use any mid-level exception,
cannot send cash in trades, and has its future first-round pick frozen; staying
over the second apron moves that pick to the end of the first round.

## Maximum player salary

A player's maximum starting salary is a percentage of the cap based on years of
service: 25% for 0-6 years, 30% for 7-9 years, and 35% for 10 or more years.
Raises are capped at 8% per year for players re-signing with their own team using
Bird rights, and 5% otherwise.

## Rookie scale contracts

First-round picks sign four-year rookie-scale deals: two guaranteed years plus
team options for years three and four. The dollar figures are fixed by draft slot.

## Designated player and supermax

A team may designate one player on a rookie extension for a five-year deal. A
player with 7-8 or 9 years of service who meets performance criteria (an All-NBA
selection, Defensive Player of the Year, or MVP in the relevant window) can sign
a "supermax" starting at 35% of the cap with their own team.

## Bird rights

A team that has held a player's contract for three seasons without that player
clearing waivers earns "full Bird rights" and may re-sign him for up to the max
even while over the cap. Two seasons earns "Early Bird" rights (a smaller
exception); one season earns "Non-Bird" rights.

## Contract length

A player re-signing with his own team can sign for up to five years. A player
signing with a new team can sign for up to four years.

## Free agency

Players become unrestricted free agents when their contract ends and they have
four or more years of service, or their team declines to make a qualifying offer.
Restricted free agents can negotiate with other teams, but the original team may
match any offer sheet within a set window.

## Two-way contracts

Each team may carry three two-way players who split time between the NBA team and
its G League affiliate. Two-way players are limited in how many days they can
spend with the NBA team and are not eligible for the playoff roster unless
converted to a standard contract.

## Player mobility and trade restrictions

A player who signs as a free agent generally cannot be traded for three months or
until December 15, whichever is later. Players with a no-trade clause (rare, and
requiring eight years of service and four with the current team) must consent to
any trade.

## Minimum roster and games-played rules

Teams must carry at least 14 players under standard contracts. Under the 2023 CBA,
a player must appear in at least 65 games (playing at least 20 minutes in most,
with a couple of 15-plus-minute games allowed) to be eligible for major end-of-
season awards such as MVP, All-NBA, and the scoring title.
